Learn Quantum with Phoebe

Fine print first. A data and AI course, not a physics tour and not a vendor pitch. Brindle is a retailer whose board has received a quantum pilot deck, and its data team has two questions: will this change our workload, and what do we have to do about the encryption. The answers are taught on a real statevector simulator that runs in every page, up to ten qubits and 1,024 amplitudes. Speedups are per algorithm, never general: at ten qubits a search makes 25 oracle calls where a classical one makes 512, and that is the only table where quantum wins outright. Most quantum machine learning claims die at data loading, because loading N rows costs N and a square-root search gain cannot survive it. One famous claim was dequantized outright. Noise collapses success with circuit depth, and adding qubits makes it worse. The hardware timeline is contested evidence and is taught as a disagreement. The one quantum job a data team is certain to get is the post-quantum inventory, which is a data job. Six leader sessions on the decision, ten practitioner sessions on the build, four benches that run in the page. No vendor, machine, roadmap or price is named anywhere, and nothing here is investment advice.

If you come from a data team

Leader session 6 in three columns. What a conventional data analyst, engineer or scientist already has that is worth more here, what is genuinely new, and the habits that are actively dangerous on this hardware.

Carries over

  • Sampling and standard error. A measurement is a sample; every shot count is an estimate with an error bar you already know how to compute.
  • Linear algebra. A gate is a matrix and a register is a vector; the pair loop is an index trick, not new maths.
  • Benchmark discipline. Baselines, held-out checks and the habit of writing the expected number before running anything.
  • Data inventory and lineage. Cataloguing which systems hold which data for how long is the exact shape of the post-quantum job.

Must learn

  • Amplitudes and interference. Signs that cancel are where every speedup lives, and they are never visible in a sample.
  • Per-algorithm speedup factors. Square root for unstructured search, conditional exponentials elsewhere, each with its own fine print.
  • Loading, readout and noise arithmetic. Order-N load, n-repetition readout, (1 - p) to the G survival. Three costs no deck prints.
  • The post-quantum standards. FIPS 203, 204 and 205 of August 2024, the proposed 2030 and 2035 dates, and what harvest now, decrypt later means for your archive.

Must unlearn

  • Debugging by printing state. You cannot inspect a register mid-circuit without destroying it; the only output is a sample at the end.
  • More compute is more answer. More qubits lengthens the circuit, and under any noise the answer gets worse, measurably.
  • Reading a speedup as a wall-clock claim. It is a count of oracle calls under assumptions about loading and readout; translate before you compare.
  • Treating the timeline as settled. The primary sources disagree and none gives a date; plan on the data's secrecy lifetime instead.

What is real here

Every number in the course was computed before any page quoted it. The simulator is exact; the noise is a declared model; the loading arithmetic prints its assumptions.
